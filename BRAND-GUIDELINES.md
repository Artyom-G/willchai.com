# willchai.com — Brand Guidelines

This is the living source of truth for how willchai.com should look, sound, and
feel. It translates the product decisions in `DECISIONS.md` into a coherent
identity and should guide design, copywriting, photography selection, and
future conversation-based edits. `DESIGN-SYSTEM.md` defines how the visual
ideas are implemented, while `CONTENT-GUIDE.md` defines the repeatable
publishing workflow.

## Brand foundation

willchai.com is the personal home of a student, maker, and storyteller. It is
not a conventional portfolio or a
photography-business template. The site brings Will's creative work and
professional photography together through a shared interest in people,
attention, and the ways stories are made and remembered.

“Sharing Stories” is the organising idea for the practice. It is broad enough
to hold film, writing, games, watchmaking, photography, and future forms of
work without making those disciplines seem unrelated. It should appear as a
useful invitation and point of view, not as a slogan repeated on every page.

The identity should feel personal, curious, precise, and quietly confident.
Its visual world may be eccentric, organic, rough-edged, or unexpectedly
digital, but it should never feel careless. The goal is upscale editorial
attention without fashion-magazine posturing; retro character without
nostalgic costume; and digital experimentation without making the interface
difficult to use. The calm system must occasionally be interrupted by
something unmistakably authored, because uninterrupted polish reads as a
technology company rather than a person.

## What the brand should communicate

A first-time visitor should understand that there is a real person behind the
site, that his interests are unusually broad but connected, and that the work
has been selected with care. The experience should create curiosity before it
tries to explain everything. It can reward exploration, but the paths into the
work and the route to contact should remain immediate and legible.

Photography is the professional service and receives one substantial page with
its own client inquiry path. Ten to fifteen featured photographs flow through
one uncategorized editorial sequence. Small captions identify a person,
occasion, place, or commission when confirmed. Pricing and selected
professional relationships follow the feature sequence, then a caption free
shuffle carries the wider body of work into the complete service footer. Films
and Projects are separate destinations. Individual work pages belong within
those two areas. Sharing Stories continues as the idea connecting the practice.
The whole site uses the broad invitation “Let’s do something together,” while
Photography provides a more specific inquiry route.

Films uses original stills and poster artwork on the shared white canvas. A
slight offset between entries gives the chooser its composition, while titles,
director credits and viewing actions stay together. Motion follows the choice
to explore a film or watch its trailer. Personal production accounts carry
the collaborative character of the work into the detail pages.

The Résumé is a professional record with matching PDF and HTML versions. It may
link to a film or project when the connection is useful. `/hey/` is a compact
utility page for quick links and QR codes. Contact appears within existing
pages through relevant invitations and compact endings.

Photography gives visual quality priority while supporting portrait and event
inquiries through useful information and straightforward contact. Current
photographs, captions, and professional relationships serve as provisional
material during design review. The page should be assessed through its full
scrolling composition and ordinary visitor tasks on desktop and mobile.

## Voice and writing

The voice is conversational, observant, assured, and specific. It should sound
like a thoughtful person explaining work he genuinely cares about, not a brand
performing cleverness or an institution describing him from a distance. Use
first person when Will is speaking about his life, process, or point of view.
Let concrete details and the work itself establish credibility; avoid inflated
claims, generic creative language, and résumé-like self-description.

The following is the canonical instruction for all AI-assisted site copy and
for the editing of human-written drafts:

> Use cohesive prose with complete sentences and substantive paragraphs. Avoid
> staccato, listicle style editorial prose. Avoid sentence fragments,
> repetitive sentence openings, anaphora, stacked formatting, isolated one or
> two word lines, label driven prose, and artificial rhythm created through
> excessive line breaks. Do not give each thought its own paragraph. Group
> related ideas into natural paragraphs. Use headings only when they genuinely
> help organize a longer response. Use lists only when enumeration materially
> improves clarity. Avoid repeating the same syntactic structure several times
> in succession. Prefer ordinary conversational prose over stylized editorial
> formatting.

This standard applies to homepage introductions, biographies, project text,
service descriptions, captions, calls to action, metadata, and AI-generated
drafts. Functional interface labels such as “Photography,” “Next image,” or
“Contact” may remain brief because they identify actions rather than imitate
editorial prose. Concision is useful, but it should come from editing complete
thoughts rather than breaking them into dramatic fragments.

## Core language

The name should normally appear as “Will Chai.” The approved homepage
introduction is “I'm a student, a maker, and a storyteller.” “Sharing Stories”
names the idea connecting the creative work. Neither line needs to be forced
into every page; repetition would make a personal idea feel like a campaign.
The primary invitation is “Let's go do something.”

Project descriptions should begin with what the work is and why it exists,
then add process, role, collaborators, or recognition when those details help
someone understand it. Photography copy should be warm and direct, with enough
practical information to make an inquiry feel easy. It should not adopt the
language of luxury services simply to appear upscale.

## Visual character

The visual identity is predominantly light, orderly, and modular. A cool
pale-grey canvas and crisp white surfaces create the stable foundation, while
near-black type and restrained rules keep it precise. The homepage project
stream is the deliberate exception: an Ink field separates its image-led
panels while the identity rail and reading surfaces stay light. Beige, cream,
parchment, sepia, and other warm paper neutrals are not part of the brand.
Warmth should come from Will's images, language, and selected project colours
rather than from a beige overlay applied to everything.

The site should feel more like a singular designed object than a standard
portfolio template. Its fixed identity rail and short scrolling project panels
create recognisable structure. Homepage project panels share one exterior size
but develop different identities through real imagery, crop, embedded artwork,
typographic placement, and interaction. They use edge-to-edge media rather than
different solid background colours. Desktop panels are 36% of the viewport
height and never exceed 40%, allowing several parts of the practice to coexist
on screen. This rhythm is central to the identity and should survive individual
redesigns.

Retro influence should come from typographic character, proportion, confident
flat colours, and slightly unexpected composition. Pixel-derived edges,
coarse digital marks, irregular silhouettes, and rough interruptions may be
used as deliberate exceptions, but not as a theme pasted onto every module.
Avoid fake ageing, computer-terminal references, vintage office ephemera, and
literal imitation of an old interface. The result can be strange and playful,
while the underlying grid, navigation, and reading experience remain calm.

## Colour

The permanent palette is deliberately compact. Cloud (`#EEF0F2`) is the page
canvas, White (`#FFFFFF`) is the principal reading surface, Ink (`#111317`) is
the main text colour, and Slate (`#5F6670`) carries secondary text. The slight
blue bias keeps the neutral system crisp and prevents it from drifting towards
beige.

Two accents provide a recognisable vocabulary. Brand Blue (`#2D29F4`) is the
primary interactive and identity colour. Vermilion (`#D9362B`) may define a
selective structural line or support an occasional editorial detail. Ink is
the traditional high-contrast highlight; there is no separate playful acid
colour. Use white text on Brand Blue or Vermilion. These pairings meet
normal-text contrast; do not improvise lower-contrast combinations for
functional copy.

Photography and project imagery supply most of the site's visual richness. Homepage
project panels do not receive different solid brand fills, and the accents do
not map permanently to disciplines. A colour sampled from project imagery may
appear in a small label, rule, or detail, but it should not replace the image as
the panel's identity. Gradients, glass effects, faux paper, and decorative
texture are outside the structural interface. The holographic contact ticket is
an established exception. The approved full-panel gravitational-lensing
interaction is a second bounded exception. Neither treatment should become a
general page background.

## Typography

The foundation typeface is **Funnel Sans**. Use it for body copy, navigation,
captions, metadata, controls, and small headings. Its mixture of square and
circular construction gives the interface character while remaining clear in
substantive paragraphs and at small sizes. Use weight 400 for reading text, 500
for recurring interface elements, and 600 only when a heading needs firmer
hierarchy.

The display typeface is **Unbounded**. Use its regular 400 weight for major page
titles, selected project titles, and occasional short pull lines. Its width and
unconventional silhouette supply the retro, experimental quality that the
neutral interface should not attempt to create through decoration. Do not use
Unbounded for paragraphs, long positioning lines, metadata, or controls. Scale
it down before allowing a title to create an awkward wrap.

The existing logo uses **Plantagenet Cherokee**. Preserve that lettering as an
approved outlined SVG or other fixed artwork rather than loading Plantagenet as
live website text. The font is proprietary and should not be copied into the
site or served as a webfont without a separate licence that explicitly permits
it. Give the logo asset an accessible name and retain a high-quality editable
source outside the deployment folder.

Live website text contains only Funnel Sans and Unbounded; a third live-text
font is not needed. Individual projects do not introduce additional fonts,
although lettering that already exists inside the logo, posters, titles, or
project artwork may remain part of that media. Both live-text families are
released under the SIL Open Font License. Self-host subsetted WOFF2 files and
retain the licence text in the repository so the published site does not depend
on a third-party font request. Monospaced type is not used for recurring roles.

Typography relies on scale, spacing, and contrast rather than an excessive
number of weights or styles. Sentence case is the default. All-caps text is
limited to very short functional markers and is never the site's general tone.
No live website text may be smaller than 14 pixels at the default browser
scale. Fifteen pixels is preferred for navigation, counters, captions, and
section numbers; substantive body copy begins around 17 pixels.

## Photography and imagery

Images should make the identity feel true rather than polished into
anonymity. The portrait cycler can show formal, candid, playful, and working
sides of Will, provided every image belongs to the same honest portrait set.
The interaction needs a visible cue so visitors understand that the image can
change without relying on hover alone.

Published portfolio imagery should favour real photographs, frames, sketches,
objects, and process material from the work. Avoid stock imagery and do not use
generated images as substitutes for undocumented projects or real portraits.
Select fewer, stronger images and give each an intentional crop. The interface
supports the photography; it does not apply a decorative effect that makes
unrelated work look artificially uniform.

## Motion and interaction

The site earns its wow factor through a small number of responsive details:
the changing portrait, the black-hole panel entry, expressive focus and hover
states, and transitions that preserve a sense of place when entering a project.
The black hole uses a near-black event horizon with no circular photon ring, a
thin warm accretion trace, and radial deflection of the project imagery rather
than a colourful UI vortex, chromatic filter, or circular magnifier. The full panel is its rendering surface,
but the visual phenomenon remains local to the pointer and behind all live panel
copy. Its disk responds between roughly −20 and +20 degrees as a pointer moves
across a panel, while tightly spaced bold Unbounded “ENTER” lettering bends
coherently inside the horizon. Every non-hovered project panel receives a 20%
Black veil while a fine pointer engages one panel, concentrating attention
without altering the panel's underlying artwork.
The position follows with damped spring weight instead of snapping to the cursor.
Motion should remain purposeful and reversible, and reduced-motion visitors
receive the direct link without spectacle.

Continuous floating, scroll-jacking, ornamental loading sequences, custom
cursor replacement, and ambient heavy animation are not part of the identity. The
holographic contact ticket may use pointer-responsive three-dimensional tilt
and a short click reaction because the movement communicates that the object
can be handled. Preserve the live site's original slow easing, large hover
scale, ±20-degree pointer tilt, periodic horizontal jiggle, and click bounce.
The light source tracks the pointer, while the repeated W layer is rotated in
three dimensions behind the foil.
The black-hole entry is the second bounded exception. It is
created only when a panel is engaged, uses one reusable rendering surface, has
no continuous idle loop, and limits pointer updates to the display's animation
frames. Every interaction must work
with a keyboard, remain understandable on touchscreens, and respect
reduced-motion preferences.

## Shape, spacing, and graphic details

The homepage project stream uses consistent Black gutters around unframed media
panels. The three-pixel Vermilion dashed line remains a selective accent for
other meaningful interactive or editorial regions, but it does not divide the
identity rail from the homepage work. Light reading modules may still use the
Cloud canvas. Avoid shadows.

Spacing follows an eight-pixel rhythm with a four-pixel half step. The working
scale is 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, and 96 pixels. Desktop panel gutters are
16 pixels; narrow screens use 12 pixels. Internal panel padding begins at 24
pixels on narrow screens and 32 pixels on desktop, increasing to 48 or 64
pixels only when a sparse composition benefits from it.

The sidebar identity lockup gives the `willchai.com` name greater presence than
ordinary navigation: use 20px on desktop and 18px on narrow layouts. The label
for the general destination is simply “Contact”; the warmer invitation belongs
inside the contact section rather than in the navigation.

Rules, arrows, counters, and small geometric marks should be drawn with CSS or
simple SVG rather than sourced from a general-purpose icon library. A two-pixel
Brand Blue focus ring with a three-pixel offset is the standard keyboard focus
treatment. These details should feel direct and graphic, not skeuomorphic.

The portrait uses a large, classic pixel-stepped arrow as its click cue.
Only the default formal portrait uses a circular crop; the three candid
alternate portraits keep square corners and their native aspect ratios. The
control contains no explanatory text or image count. Homepage sections also avoid
eyebrow labels, scroll instructions, and other helper copy when the structure
already makes the action clear.

The system needs an authored exception layer, but its specific visual language
remains open. An interruption should come from Will's existing objects,
artwork, handwriting, imagery, or project processes rather than a generic blob,
sticker, or pixel badge invented to make the page appear playful. Each major
view should normally contain one dominant exception rather than distributing
eccentric decoration evenly. It must preserve readable text, obvious
interaction, keyboard access, and a stable mobile layout.

The existing contact ticket is the primary example. Its perforated silhouette,
pointer-responsive tilt, 1.1× hover scale, blue holographic activation,
recessed repeated-W texture, periodic jiggle, and click-to-copy email action
are carried directly from the live site. It works
as a memorable contact invitation at the end of a page and may inspire a small
number of related tactile objects, but the full holographic treatment remains
specific to contact.

Photography uses Will's approved 32 × 32 pixel artwork for brief appearances.
The opening enlarges the artwork in whole pixel steps above the Featured
photographs boundary, then lets the character duck behind it after four seconds. The pricing scene gives the character a physical action by pushing
a decorative Gold tier card away to reveal the shoot builder. The homepage
pointer and irregular blue shape connect this scene to Will's existing work.

The current blue W in `system/assets/willchai-mark.avif` is the official compact mark and
should appear in the system library, site metadata, and suitable identity or
footer contexts. “willchai.com” remains the official website name. The W mark
and the Plantagenet Cherokee wordmark are complementary assets rather than
interchangeable typography.

Interface responses use a 160-millisecond transition with an ease-out curve;
image changes use 360 milliseconds. Movement is normally limited to opacity,
crop, colour, or a translation of no more than eight pixels. Reduced-motion
mode removes translations and changes content without an animated delay.

Photography opens with a linked Will Chai name, the Photography title, a short
portrait and event description, and a single booking link. Featured photographs
form a native horizontal strip with close spacing and complete image proportions.
The next photograph remains partly visible. Previous and Next move the strip,
while View together exposes the full collection. Keyboard arrows, Home and End
operate the focused strip. Vertical document scrolling remains native.

Will supplied Jitter references for Orbit: Social Media Showreel, Share Your Work,
and Image Stretch Transition. The current study gathers the visible photographs
into a brief overlapping arrangement, then opens them into the strip. Horizontal
movement produces a bounded elastic response. Opening and changing a photograph
uses a directional image stretch and masked reveal. Each image returns to its
native proportions at rest. Movement ends when browsing settles, and direct
interaction can finish the introductory composition immediately.

The opening character emerges from the lower edge of the first photograph after
the collection settles, takes a photograph, and ducks behind the edge. The four
second greeting uses the approved four poses and integer enlargement. The later
appearance retains the approved pushing poses. A blue rim joins the enclosure
sides beneath the Gold tier prop. Will pushes the panel past the edge, recovers
his stance, pauses, and ducks vertically behind the rim. The scene stays opaque
until his exit is complete, then reveals the board. The prop reads Standard
event package. Direct inquiry links and form interaction reveal the usable board
immediately. Replay remains available.

The white shoot board retains its irregular blue enclosure. An editable shoot
name stays above two questions about place and date. Next reveals an optional
CAD budget and additional notes, then the completed inquiry with Open email and
Copy text. Back preserves every answer. The rough pointer accompanies Next and
responds briefly to a press. A directional mask exchanges the question pair while
the title and photographic preview stay anchored. Supplied amounts are labelled
Your budget, and visitors may request guidance. Actual fees are quoted personally.

An opened photograph offers Add reference across the featured work and shuffle.
Selections appear on the shoot board and remain removable. Their image links
appear in the same generated message used by email and copying. Collection
headers provide a return to the shoot when references are selected. Clipboard
failure selects the message for manual copying. Inquiry contents stay in the
current page.

Organization marks retain their measured artwork sizing. The shuffle reserves
image dimensions and fills continuous columns while preserving earlier positions.
The footer retains the original contact ticket, policy, rights and homepage return.
Reduced motion presents the contact sheet, direct image changes and usable builder.
The composition is prepared for visual review. Photograph selection, captions,
organizations and actual pricing remain provisional.

## Stewardship

Brand consistency means preserving the underlying voice and logic, not making
every project look identical. New work may introduce an accent colour, crop
behaviour, display-scale treatment, or panel composition when the choice
reflects that project. It should still use the shared navigation, two-family
live-text system, spacing, accessibility rules, and light modular canvas.

When a lasting brand decision changes, record it in `DECISIONS.md` and update
this guide in the same change. When the decision affects reusable tokens or
components, update `DESIGN-SYSTEM.md`; when it affects the way work is written
or entered, update `CONTENT-GUIDE.md`. If two documents conflict, the most
recent dated entry in the decision log governs until the other guide is
reconciled.

## Open brand decisions

The first portrait set and assignment of accent colours to launch projects
remain open. The compact W mark is available, while the final production
wordmark asset should preserve the existing Plantagenet Cherokee lettering as
vector outlines. These remaining choices should be decided with real copy and
working browser specimens rather than generated lettering.
